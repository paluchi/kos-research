Enterprise & governance cluster in regulated verticals — finance, insurance, healthcare, manufacturing (driven by compliance).

AI-native startups concentrate on Tech / SaaS GTM & finance teams — their easy early adopters. A few go vertical: Athena (legal/finance), NLSQL (healthcare), Spotfire (pharma/industrial), Quadratic (fintech).

Customer geography skews US-first (~70%+); OSS (Metabase, Superset) over-indexes in India, Brazil & LATAM.

A US-built, SF-centered, finance-&-SaaS-targeted market — with verticals largely unclaimed.

The crowded center is where companies go to die.

How They Die — Three Failure Modes

Every corpse: a tiny team with a narrow, commoditizable core

Died — no moat
DataGPT (pricing suicide — killed its $99 tier), Zing (couldn't raise), Trevor (no AI, stagnant).
Acqui-hired for tech
Seek→IBM, Outerbase→Cloudflare, Redash→Databricks, PopSQL→Timescale, Hyperquery→Deepnote. Bought for parts.
Consolidated into a suite
Rows→Superhuman/Coda — profitable, 2.2M users, still shut down. Even winners get absorbed.
The unifying lesson: narrow + tiny + commoditizable (text-to-SQL / SQL editor / spreadsheet) = dead or bought for parts. Best-in-class accuracy didn't save them — Seek had a world-record SQL model and still got absorbed.

How Competitors Build a Moat

What actually holds — and what doesn't

Durable moats
  • Distribution / installed base — Power BI, Tableau, Looker, Metabase. Neutralize AI via reach + price.
  • Data gravity — Cortex, Genie, BigQuery, SAP. "Your data already lives here." (Also their cage.)
  • Vertical / domain depth — Spotfire (pharma), Athena (legal/finance).
  • Workflow stickiness — Sigma apps, Retool tools, Domo pipelines.
Weak / failed moats
  • Proprietary accuracy model — Seek, Defog. Commoditizes fast; both bought/tiny.
  • Semantic-layer ownership — being auto-generated & commoditized (Atlan, Cube).
  • Open-source community — adoption ≠ revenue ("stars ≠ business").
  • "We do NL/AI/MCP" — table stakes a solo dev ships.

The real moats are distribution, data-gravity, vertical depth & platform-breadth.

What KOS Should Be

The landscape dictates it: KOS sits at the center everyone is racing to, so it can't be built on features. It must be built on the edges incumbents structurally can't follow.

Be source-agnostic
NoSQL + files + docs + heterogeneous — warehouse-locked rivals can't copy without leaving their cage. The one wedge that survives against everyone.
Be a platform, not a feature
Ask + Act + Present + Learn. The graveyard's verdict: narrow = acqui-hired; broad = survives.
Stay accessible
Never kill the affordable tier (DataGPT's cause of death). Enterprise-only minnows die; the consumer/accessible ones (Julius) thrive.
Earn trust without the tax
No semantic-layer authoring — answer trust with confidence + transparency (Seek's way), or plug into Cube/Atlan via MCP.

Source-agnostic + unstructured, packaged as an accessible ask-AND-act platform — not a warehouse-locked NL feature.

The Landscape in One Line

The center — natural-language analytics — is a red ocean everyone is flooding into.

The durable moats are distribution, data-gravity, vertical depth & platform-breadth — and KOS can only hold the last two.

So KOS's survival = source-agnostic + unstructured breadth, as an accessible ask-and-act platform — ideally pointed at a vertical it can own.
